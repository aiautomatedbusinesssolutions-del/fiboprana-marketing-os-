NEVER:
- Claim to cure, treat, heal, diagnose, or prevent any disease or condition
- Make health-outcome claims ("reduces anxiety/depression," "lowers cortisol," "clinically proven," "doctor-recommended," "medical-grade")
- Claim to measure or detect stress, emotions, consciousness, or mental states ("accurately measures your stress," "knows your emotions") — avoid "measure" as a verb in public copy entirely
- Make uniqueness claims ("the only," "first ever," "uncontested," "no one else does this") — the mechanic is contested
- Make absolute market claims ("nothing tracks the mind") — scope to the reader's own devices instead ("your ring tracks your body, not your mind")
- Claim capabilities that don't ship yet (live device integrations, named partners) until they actually launch
- Use scoring/grading/optimization framing: "score," "rank," "grade," "you're at X%," "optimize," "streak," "crush your goals," "biohack," leaderboard or daily-verdict framing
- Use urgency, scarcity, FOMO, "risk-free!" or any dark-pattern mechanic; never imply we sell, share, or mine mind-state data
- Promise or guarantee results; never use outcome testimonials without substantiation on file
- Cite HRV-biofeedback research as marketing proof (it implies a health-benefit claim)
- Lead with energy/chakra/manifestation framing for the general audience (prana vocabulary belongs in the practitioner layer, where it's literal to a practice)
ALWAYS:
- Stay in the general-wellness lane: "may help you notice," "patterns," "trends," "over time," "your own signals"
- Frame "is it working" as experience, not efficacy — pair it with "you decide what it means" and stay honest about uncertainty; it never resolves into "it works"
- Treat the reader as smart; relieve, never pressure — "you're not behind"
